## What we hold

For each paper, ReOpSy holds bibliographic metadata (title, authors, year,
venue, identifiers, link) and a short summary generated automatically from the
paper's abstract. Where — and only where — the source declares an open licence
permitting reproduction, we also hold the abstract as published.

We do not hold full texts, figures, tables or supplementary material.

## Who can file a notice

The rights holder in a work, or a person authorised to act on their behalf. An
author may also request removal of their own work regardless of who holds the
copyright.

## What to include

A notice we can act on contains:

1. **Identification of the material** — the paper title as it appears in the
   app, and the DOI or arXiv identifier if you have it.
2. **Your relationship to the work** — rights holder, author, or authorised
   agent, and for an agent, evidence of authority.
3. **The basis of the request** — for example: the abstract is displayed but the
   licence does not permit it; the metadata is wrong; the work has been
   retracted; you wish the work delisted.
4. **Contact details** — a name and an address we can reply to.
5. **A statement** that the information in the notice is accurate and that you
   have a good-faith belief the use complained of is not authorised.

Please send a notice rather than a court order in the first instance. Almost
everything here can be fixed in a day.

## What we do, and when

| Timescale | Action |
|---|---|
| Within 2 working days | We acknowledge receipt. |
| Within 7 working days | We act on the notice or explain why we have not. |

**Where an abstract is displayed without a licence permitting it, we remove the
abstract immediately on notice, without waiting for the review to conclude.**
That is a one-line database change and there is no reason to leave it in place
while we correspond.

Possible outcomes:

- **Correction** — we fix the metadata and, if the abstract came from an
  incorrect licence field, re-derive the record.
- **Summary-only** — we remove the abstract and keep the title, authors and
  link. A bibliographic reference to a published work is not ordinarily an
  infringement, and delisting a paper entirely reduces its discoverability,
  which is usually not what an author wants.
- **Full delisting** — we remove the record and add its identifier to a
  suppression list so a later ingest run does not restore it.

## Counter-notice

If your work was removed and you believe that was a mistake, write to the same
address with the identifier and your reasoning. We will restore records removed
in error.

## Retractions

If a paper has been retracted, tell us and we will delist it. We are not able to
monitor retraction notices systematically at present.

## Repeat and bulk requests

A publisher wishing to exclude a corpus, or a repository wishing to change how
its records are treated, can write to the same address. We would rather agree a
standing rule than process notices one at a time.
