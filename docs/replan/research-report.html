ReOpSy · research and replan · 3 October 2026

From a paper feed to a literature-review habit

ReOpSy already has a working Expo app, Google sign-in and a nightly pipeline. The research says the idea is right but the build is aimed at the wrong target. Researchers don't need another scroll of papers. They need a short daily deck they can trust, a way to remember what they saved, and a literature review that builds itself while they read.

The short version

  • Fix feed quality first. In the feed the app ships today, 17 of 47 cards match their topic, and none has a working AI summary.
  • Change the loop from an endless feed to Triage → Recall → Synthesize: a finite daily deck, three recall questions, and a literature matrix you can export. No competitor offers that loop as a daily mobile habit.
  • Rebuild the backend for ₹0: GitHub Actions writes static JSON to Cloudflare Pages. Render, SQLite and the Supabase leftovers go.
  • Keep the flashcard format, Expo app, Google sign-in, streak logic and BibTeX export.
  • Spend: ₹2,200 once for the Play account. Everything else fits inside free tiers with room to grow.

01 · Audit of the current build

Where the app stands today

I read the repository at commit 0801249 and measured the feed the app bundles today (app/src/data/dailyFeed.json, generated 22 August 2026, 47 cards across 5 topics).

47/47"Catchy titles" identical to the original title. The LLM step is failing silently.
32/47Summaries are abstracts cut off mid-sentence and ending in "…".
17/47Cards that mention their own topic's keywords at all.

The keyword check is rough. It marked one useful paper as off-topic (an MRI study that uses autism cohorts), and v2 would show that paper with a "why you're seeing this" note. The other misses are real: an exoplanet thesis appears under both AI in Mental Health and Autism.

Root causes

  • Topic filters. "AI in Mental Health" and "Autism Diagnosis" use the same OpenAlex filter, concepts.id:C119857082 (Machine learning). OpenAlex Concepts are deprecated and frozen, so new works are no longer tagged with them. The replacement is Topics, a 4-level hierarchy of about 4,500 topics.
  • No quality gates. The shipped feed includes a thesis whose "abstract" is an embargo notice (dated 2027) and a self-published Zenodo record with fringe physics claims (dated 2029). The future-date filter from a recent commit doesn't catch OpenAlex records.
  • Four places that hold the feed: SQLite on Render's ephemeral disk, a Firestore feeds/latest doc, an unused Supabase schema, and a JSON file bundled into the app.
  • User-supplied LLM keys. Custom topics ask users to paste their own Gemini/Mistral/Grok keys, which are stored on the device and called from the client.
  • Privacy declaration drift. The launch guide says to answer "no data collected" on Play's Data Safety form. Google sign-in plus Firestore sync is data collection, so that answer is now wrong.
  • Repo weight. More than 50 agent work folders, compiled test output and 15 SQLite test databases are tracked in git.

04 · From complaint to rule

Lessons learned and the rules ReOpSy adopts

ThemeLessonRule or feature in v2
1. NoiseVolume reads as spam. Relevance must be visible and correctable.Topic preview before subscribing. "Why you're seeing this" on every card. "Not relevant" with a reason retrains the ranking. A precision gate of 0.8 or better on a hand-labelled gold set before a topic ships.
2. SpamEvery unwanted ping erodes trust in the next one.At most one local reminder a day, at a time the user picks. Opt-in after the first finished deck. Auto-pause after 3 ignored days. No invites, no fake social pings.
3. PaywallsDiscovering a paper you can't read is a broken promise."Open PDF" or "Paywalled" chip before the tap; Unpaywall best open location; an open-access-only filter.
4. Billing trapsOne billing scandal outweighs years of goodwill.Reading is free forever. Play Billing only. Renewal reminder 7 days ahead. 2-tap cancel. No trial that converts silently.
5. CreditsMetered anxiety kills exploration.Summaries are precomputed once per paper and free to read. Pro is a flat price.
6. Wrong summariesLLMs overgeneralize by default, and fake links destroy credibility.Structured summary with a mandatory Limits field. Every number checked against the abstract. Hype words blocked. Links built only from source IDs. "AI-written" label, a one-tap report, and the abstract one tap away.
7. FiltersResearchers think in year, venue and type.Library filters: topic, year, preprint or peer-reviewed, open PDF, has code.
8. Data lossThe library is the user's work. Losing it ends the relationship.Local-first storage; sync only ever merges; export any time.
9. ShutdownsSingle-source products die with their source.Several CC0-first sources behind one adapter interface, and a static cache. The pipeline is open source.
10. Streak pressureLoss framing works until it backfires.Earned freezes, a weekly rhythm view, neutral copy after a missed day, milestones for output.
11. Addictive designRegulators now name infinite scroll and engagement-tuned feeds.A finite deck with a "caught up" end, no autoplay, and ranking for relevance rather than time-on-app.
12. AccessibilityDense science on small screens excludes people fast.WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes, 200% text support, a button for every gesture, rendered or stripped LaTeX.

05 · Habit without harm

Engagement mechanics that are fair and useful

The test for each mechanic: would a user who understood exactly how it works still choose it? The ones below pass. The ones on the right don't.

Use

  1. Finite deck with a "caught up" end. This is the opposite of infinite scroll, whose inventor has publicly apologised for it and which the EU now targets.
  2. A goal the user chooses (5, 10 or 15 cards). Autonomy keeps motivation intrinsic.
  3. Spaced recall: three questions a day from saved cards, at widening intervals. Meta-analyses show spaced retrieval practice beats massed review for durable learning. Reading turns into knowing.
  4. Progress toward a real artifact. "Survey: 14 of 40 papers" and "first export" are milestones. Rewards tied to output avoid the drop-off seen when points replace purpose.
  5. Visible learning from feedback. "Your feed adjusted from 12 ratings." Scholar Inbox shows that explicit ratings with active learning work.
  6. Forgiving streaks: one freeze per 7 active days, at most 2 banked, with the weekly rhythm as the main view.
  7. One labelled serendipity card per deck from an adjacent topic, which pushes back on filter bubbles.
  8. Implementation intention. The user names when they read ("after morning tea, 08:30"), and that time is the only reminder.
  9. Opt-in social (later): lab journal-club decks and share images that carry the citation.

Never

  • Infinite scroll or autoplay into more content
  • Pull-to-refresh "slot machine" rewards
  • Red badge counts and countdown timers
  • Streak-loss push notifications or guilt copy
  • Leaderboards by default
  • Like counts with no community behind them (the current likes field)
  • Paywall interrupts mid-deck
  • Trials that auto-convert without a reminder
  • Ranking for time-on-app

06 · What to build

Feature set and roadmap

Core loop: Triage a finite daily deck → Recall what you saved → Synthesize it into a literature matrix that exports to BibTeX, RIS and CSV.

Now: MVP (about 7 weeks)

  • Today deck sized by your goal; "caught up" end
  • Card v2: finding headline, 4-part summary, key numbers, chips, why-shown, AI label
  • Reading ladder: card → abstract → open PDF → note
  • Triage: Not relevant (with reason), Save, Read later, Add to survey
  • Topic catalog with a 5-paper preview
  • Library with filters; matrix; BibTeX/RIS/CSV export
  • Recall: 3 a day, Leitner intervals
  • Weekly rhythm, forgiving streak, output milestones
  • One local reminder; report-summary sheet; offline deck
  • Android plus web PWA (covers iOS)

Next: v1.x

  • Custom topics: a phrase plus seed papers, resolved by a Worker with a preview
  • Per-user ranking from on-device embeddings of your saves
  • Weekly "your field this week" digest
  • Zotero export and sync
  • Auto-filled matrix columns (task, method, dataset, metric)
  • Share-card images with citation
  • Lab and journal-club decks
  • On-device text-to-speech briefings

Later: v2

  • "Around this paper": citation neighbourhood from OpenAlex and OpenCitations
  • Gap finder across your matrix
  • Survey outline drafted only from your library, citing only those papers
  • Conference mode (OpenReview)
  • Hindi and other summary languages
  • Institutional and library plans; native iOS once revenue covers the Apple fee

08 · How it fits together

Technical architecture

The core idea: summarize each paper once, publish static files, and let every user read the same CDN. Cost then grows with the number of papers, not the number of users. No server needs to stay awake.

flowchart LR
  subgraph GH["GitHub Actions - nightly - free"]
    A["Fetch: arXiv, OpenAlex, Europe PMC, bioRxiv"] --> B["Normalize + dedupe"]
    B --> C["Quality gates: abstract, date, retraction, source type"]
    C --> D["Rank: local embeddings + topic seed papers"]
    D --> E["Summarize: LLM JSON + validators, else extractive"]
    E --> F["Enrich: Unpaywall, code links, key numbers"]
  end
  F --> G[("Cloudflare Pages: static JSON")]
  G --> H["Expo app: Android + PWA"]
  H <--> I[("expo-sqlite: local-first library")]
  H -. optional sync .-> J[("Firebase Auth + Firestore user doc")]
  H -- report summary --> K["Cloudflare Worker + D1"]
  K -- 2+ reports --> L["GitHub issue + overrides.json"]
  L --> F

In words: a nightly GitHub Actions job fetches candidates, applies quality gates, ranks them with local embeddings, summarizes the winners and writes static JSON to Cloudflare Pages. The app reads those files, stores the library on the device and optionally syncs it through Firebase. Summary reports go to a Worker, which opens an issue and feeds corrections back into the next run.

14 · Read with care

Limits of this research

  • Play Store and App Store review pages couldn't be retrieved from the research environment. Review evidence comes from Capterra (13 R Discovery reviews), Trustpilot, G2, aggregators, studies and the press. Samples are small, and Trustpilot skews toward billing complaints. The playbook includes a script to mine about 500 store reviews per app on your machine.
  • User counts for competitors are vendor claims, except Scholar Inbox's, which come from its paper.
  • Free-tier numbers were checked in October 2026, and several come from third-party trackers rather than official pages. Re-check them before relying on any single number.
  • The feed audit's keyword check is a heuristic. The gold set in Phase 1 replaces it with human labels.
  • This is product research, not legal advice. Have the privacy policy and terms reviewed before charging money.
